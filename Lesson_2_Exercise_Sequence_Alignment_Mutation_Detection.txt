STEP 1 — IDENTIFY Test_sequence_1 WITH BLASTp
--------------------------------------------------------------

Go to:     blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
Click:     Protein BLAST (BLASTp)
Paste:     Test_sequence_1 into the query box
Settings:
   Database:  Non-redundant protein sequences (nr)
   Organism:  leave empty
   Matrix:    BLOSUM62
Click:     BLAST

QUESTIONS — BLASTp
--------------------------------------------------------------

    What do these values tell you about the match?

    Test_sequence_1 most likely come from?

   Sequence type:  Protein
   Matrix:         BLOSUM62

QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. What is the overall % Identity between
    Test_sequence_2 and the Reference_sequence?

Q2. Look carefully at the alignment output.
    Can you spot the exact position where the
    two sequences differ?
    What nucleotide is present in the tumor sequence
    vs. the reference?

Q3. Is this difference a substitution, an insertion,
    or a deletion?
    How many nucleotides are involved?

Q4. This mutation affects the reading frame.
    Is it a frameshift mutation?
    What happens to the protein after a frameshift?

Q5. The reference sequence ends with: ...CCCCTGA
    The tumor sequence ends with:     ...CCCTGA
    Can you identify exactly which nucleotide is missing?
    At what position does the deletion occur?

Q6. What is the likely consequence of this mutation
    for TP53 protein function?
    Would you expect the tumor cells to have
    functional TP53? Why or why not?

Q7. This type of mutation — a single nucleotide deletion —
    is commonly found in human cancers.
    How could a clinician use this information
    for diagnosis or treatment decisions?

EXPECTED RESULTS (read after answering)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Mutation type:   Single nucleotide deletion (frameshift)
Position:        Near the 3' end of the coding sequence
Missing base:    One "C" in the ...CCCCT... stretch
Consequence:     Frameshift → altered stop codon context
                 → likely non-functional truncated protein
                 → loss of tumor suppressor activity
Clinical note:   TP53 frameshift mutations are associated
                 with Li-Fraumeni syndrome and poor
                 prognosis in multiple cancer types.
